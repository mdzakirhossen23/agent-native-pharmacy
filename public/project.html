The problem

A pharmacy owner needs one place to receive stock, track expiry, serve customers and see outstanding balances. The AI helps interpret the records and prepare a restocking proposal. The owner reviews the decision and remains in control.

What works

  • Editable medicine catalogue and manual photo upload.
  • Batch purchases, FEFO allocation and expiry blocking.
  • Cash/credit POS, receipts, returns and due ledgers.
  • AI bookkeeping answers and reviewed restocking drafts.
  • Five languages, dated BDT/USD reference conversion.
  • Dark desktop window with responsive forms.

One shared action path

The actual @agent-native/core/action defineAction implementation validates manual operations and approved AI drafts. Both use the same handlers and browser snapshot.

The server processes each request temporarily and returns the result. Durable business records remain in this browser's IndexedDB. Changes require internet connectivity. Export backups before clearing browser data.

Try the people-and-agent workflow

  1. Open the separate demo workspace. Choose Add initial catalogue to add fictional stock and original sample illustrations.
  2. Receive a fictional purchase with batch, expiry and cost.
  3. Use POS to record a small simulated sale and inspect the receipt.
  4. Open AI inventory and generate a reorder proposal.
  5. Change quantities or deselect items before approving the draft.
  6. Receive the draft separately as a delivery. Approval alone never places an order or increases stock.
  7. Ask the shop assistant to summarize sales, balances or low stock.
  8. Show Settings, browser backup controls and a second display language.

The demo workspace is isolated from the normal pharmacy workspace in this browser. Use fictional names and transactions when making a public recording.

Sources and originality

The pharmacy implementation was developed during the hackathon with AI coding assistance. It uses Agent-Native, Zod, sql.js, Vite and esbuild. No third-party POS application's source was reused. Bundled icons and sample-package illustrations are original programmatic artwork. Real uploaded photos belong to the user and are excluded from the source package.

The small starter name list links to factual manufacturer/regulator sources. It is not a complete national medicine register. The app gives no treatment, dosage or substitution advice and processes no actual payments. Gross profit excludes operating expenses and taxes.
